260623 ALCYONE FOR SALE: HISTORY OF THE CATAMARAN FROM 2004 to 2026 (ENGLISH)

NOTE: The French version contains considerably more detail on this post of the blog:

260320 ALCYONE – COMPLETE INVENTORY & HISTORY (French draft currently being updated)

1) ALCYONE'S HISTORY BEFORE 2017 

ALCYONE is hull number 26 of the series and one of the last five Outremer 55 Lights ever built. As such, she benefited from all the experience accumulated from the previous boats, both in design and construction.

She is something of a survivor, having had a difficult start to her life. The Outremer shipyard itself ultimately rescued her and brought her back from the Indian Ocean after six years of operation, demonstrating its confidence in both the strength of her structure and the quality of the design.

The hull identification engraved on the starboard hull confirms that ALCYONE was the last Outremer 55 Light launched during the lifetime of designer Gérard Danson.

2003–2004: A Dream Shared by Four Partners

Delivered in April 2004, the catamaran was commissioned by four legal professionals (lawyers and notaries) who planned to operate her as a charter yacht in tropical waters.

To support this objective, she was configured with:

  • Simple systems that could be maintained anywhere in the world.
  • Conventional shaft-drive propulsion.
  • Large water capacity for extended autonomy.
  • Four spacious and symmetrical guest cabins.
  • Crew berths in the forward compartments.
  • Discreetly integrated equipment intended for charter use.
  • A simple, robust sail inventory.
  • A high-performance SSB radio system for long-distance communications.

After several cruises in the Mediterranean, ALCYONE departed for the Seychelles via the Red Sea.

2004–2008: The Reality of Charter Operations in the Seychelles

Following only limited charter activity, the project was largely abandoned.

Eventually the Outremer shipyard decided to bring the boat back to France. This proved beneficial for everyone involved:

  • The original owners recovered part of their investment.
  • Outremer acquired the vessel at a reasonable cost.
  • Future owners—including ourselves—benefited from the opportunity.
  • And the boat itself was given a second life.

One may wonder whether such an operation would still be possible today.

2009: Return to La Grande Motte via South Africa

In her existing condition, ALCYONE was delivered back to France by Jean-Pierre Balmes, Outremer's in-house skipper, assisted by two friends from the Seychelles.

The route took her via Cape Town and Brazil before arriving at La Grande Motte.

2010–2011: Major Refit at Outremer

The boat was hauled out at the Outremer yard for a major refit before being offered for sale.

The 2015 survey report (available upon request) specifically references the extensive 2011 refit carried out by the shipyard.

2012: Purchased by François for a New Family Adventure

François purchased ALCYONE directly from the Outremer yard, beginning the second chapter of her life.

2013–2014: Atlantic and Pacific Crossing via Panama

Launched in May, François and his two children undertook an extraordinary voyage covering nearly half a circumnavigation:

  • Canary Islands
  • Cape Verde
  • St Martin and the Caribbean
  • Mexico
  • Panama Canal
  • Galápagos Islands
  • Marquesas Islands
  • Fakarava (Tuamotu Islands)

The boat then spent:

  • Two months ashore in Tahiti for maintenance and a return trip to France.
  • Three months ashore at Norsand Boatyard in Whangarei, New Zealand, for similar maintenance work.

2015: New Zealand Refit and New Caledonia Season

  • First three months of the year ashore at Norsand Boatyard, Whangarei.
  • Relaunched in April.
  • Cruising season in New Caledonia.
  • Returned to Whangarei in September.
  • The owner put the boat up for sale and returned to France.

2016: Storage Ashore in New Zealand

ALCYONE remained stored ashore at Norsand Boatyard in Whangarei throughout 2016 while awaiting her next owner.

We eventually purchased her in January 2017.


With 40 knots of wind off San Francisco: strong wind, flat water, third reef, 18–22 knots of boat speed.

3)TWO INCIDENTS / 9 YEARS OF CRUISING 

1) FIJI, 2017 – STARBOARD BOW CRASH BOX DAMAGE
-   Cause: A manoeuvre was delegated to a crew member who was not yet familiar with disengaging the autopilot.
-   Repair: Temporary repairs were carried out locally, followed by a professional repair at Whangarei Boatyard two months later.
(See blog entry: "1709 – Crash at Musket Cove"

2) OFSHORE NEW ZEALAND, 2017 – MARTINGALE FAILURE DURING A SOLO PASSAGE
-   Location: Approximately 100 metres from the New Zealand coast after a solo passage
-   Cause: The Code 0 tack had been left secured on the bowsprit as weather conditions deteriorated overnight. A breaking wave loaded the sail and damaged the aluminium martingale structure.
-   Lesson learned: The tack line could have been recovered in a few seconds before conditions worsened. As every sailor knows, whether sailing alone or fully crewed, negligence is always paid for immediately at sea.
-   Repair: The final 100 miles were completed under engine power. The entire standing rigging was subsequently renewed at Whangarei.
(See blog entry: "1711 – Rough Solo Return to New Zealand")


4) IMPROVEMENTS OVER €500 SINCE 2021 

Bertrand and I agreed that ALCYONE should remain exceptionally well maintained,     stay permanently based in French Polynesia, and be improved every year.                        This has been achieved by reinvesting the savings generated through:                                • Continuous occupation and year-round onboard presence.                                                • Lower insurance costs (ALCYONE carries third-party liability insurance only).            • Reduced haul-out expenses (one haul-out every two years in Tahiti or Raiatea).              • Minimal marina fees (rarely used).                                                                                      

Prior to 2021, ALCYONE had already sailed extensively offshore. Since then, she has      spent even more time at anchor in remote atolls. This explains certain recurring.              replacements, such as the anchor chain. The cost of 60 metres of chain is roughly.             equivalent to one week in a marina, and the old chain is recycled onto our permanent    mooring.
